\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{HateXAI}, a prototype for hate speech detection with token attribution and an explicit human-review outcome. We fit a calibrated word and character TF-IDF classifier on the English HateXplain corpus. Duplicate-aware preparation preserves all published test IDs, removes overlapping development examples, and keeps duplicate groups together during calibration. On 1,924 held-out posts, the baseline reaches 0.792 macro F1 and 0.832 accuracy. At the binary decision threshold, the false-negative rate is 0.362. A cost-weighted policy selected on validation routes 14.8\% of test posts to human review. The application exposes LIME and occlusion attributions. Multilingual classification, vision fusion, and preference optimization of generated explanations remain unevaluated extensions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The scale of online conversation has outpaced manual moderation. Automated hate-speech detectors are widely deployed, yet they remain brittle: they are trained mostly on English, they ignore images and thread context, and they behave as black boxes \cite{fortuna2018survey,davidson2017hate,vidgen2020directions}. Errors are not symmetric. False negatives leave targeted groups unprotected; false positives silence minorities whose dialect or reclaimed language is misread as abuse \cite{sap2019racial}.

Low-resource and code-mixed settings make the problem harder. HASOC evaluates hate speech and offensive content across English and Indo-Aryan languages \cite{mandl2021hasoc}. Memes add a second channel in which the text is innocuous but the image supplies the attack \cite{kiela2020hatefulmemes}.

We address three gaps together:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Representation.} Model a post as \(x_i=\{T_i,V_i,C_i\}\) and learn \(f_\theta(x_i)\rightarrow y_i\).
    \item \textbf{Transparency.} Attach token-level and natural-language explanations whose faithfulness can be measured \cite{ribeiro2016lime,jacovi2020faithfulness}.
    \item \textbf{Alignment.} Use preference optimization \cite{rafailov2023dpo} so generated rationales match moderator-preferred explanations rather than fluent but unfaithful text.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A reproducible English HateXplain baseline with duplicate-aware splits, calibration and a validation-selected human-review policy.
(2) A serving implementation that exposes LIME and occlusion attributions and computes comprehensiveness and sufficiency with token masking.
(3) An inspection-driven audit that identifies and corrects concrete defects, including a self-referential reward score and a misaligned DPO sequence log-probability, with regression tests.
(4) An author-constructed adversarial probe set targeting quoted hate, counterspeech, reclaimed slurs, sarcasm, and non-hateful profanity, with contested cases flagged.
(5) A gated multimodal architecture and a DPO rationale-alignment recipe for future evaluation.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Hate speech detection.}
Early systems used bag-of-words and character n-grams \cite{davidson2017hate,zampieri2019olid}. Transformer models such as HateBERT improve in-domain English accuracy \cite{caselli2021hatebert} but transfer poorly to code-mixed Hindi--English. HateXplain adds human rationales, enabling explanation-aware training \cite{mathew2021hatexplain}. Implicit hate---abuse that avoids an explicit slur---remains difficult and is benchmarked separately \cite{elsherief2021latent}.

\paragraph{Multilingual and multimodal abuse.}
XLM-R provides strong cross-lingual initialization \cite{conneau2020xlmr}, and production systems now deploy character-level multilingual transformers \cite{lees2022perspective}. HASOC and related FIRE tasks target Indo-European hate and offense \cite{mandl2019hasoc}. The Hateful Memes challenge showed that unimodal models miss image--text interactions \cite{kiela2020hatefulmemes,gomez2020exploring}; CLIP supplies aligned visual features \cite{radford2021clip}.

\paragraph{Explainability.}
LIME \cite{ribeiro2016lime} and SHAP \cite{lundberg2017shap} approximate local decision surfaces. Faithfulness concerns whether highlighted tokens drove the prediction \cite{jacovi2020faithfulness}. The application can measure comprehensiveness as the probability drop after masking its highest-ranked evidence.

\paragraph{Reward-optimized LLMs.}
RLHF trains a reward model and then a policy \cite{ouyang2022instructgpt}. DPO skips explicit RL by optimizing a classification loss on preference pairs \cite{rafailov2023dpo}. The proposed DPO stage would update a rationale generation policy while the classifier weights stay fixed.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let a social media post be
\begin{equation}
x_i = \{T_i, V_i, C_i\},
\end{equation}
where \(T_i\) is textual content (possibly multilingual or code-mixed), \(V_i\) is an image or meme, and \(C_i\) is context (thread, page, or event metadata). The target is
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(x_i) \rightarrow y_i \in \{0,1\},
\end{equation}
with \(y_i=1\) denoting hateful content. We additionally produce an explanation \(e_i\) and a reward \(r(x_i,e_i,y_i)\).

We follow the distinction between hate speech and other offensive language \cite{davidson2017hate}. Human-reviewed preference pairs for the rationale policy remain to be collected.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Preprocessing}
URLs and mentions are canonicalized. Language ID and a script-mixing heuristic flag code-mixed posts. Text and context are concatenated with explicit markers so the encoder can attend to \(C_i\) separately from \(T_i\).

\subsection{Multimodal classifier}
XLM-R encodes \(T_i\) and \(C_i\). Optional CLIP image features encode \(V_i\) and are frozen to limit compute. A gated fusion block learns non-negative weights \((\alpha_t,\alpha_v,\alpha_c)\) over projected modalities:
\begin{equation}
h = \alpha_t\,W_t t + \alpha_v\,W_v v + \alpha_c\,W_c c.
\end{equation}
A small MLP maps \(h\) to class logits. Training uses class-weighted cross-entropy, AdamW, and linear warmup.

\subsection{Explainable AI}
For a post \(x\), LIME perturbs tokens and fits a local linear model \cite{ribeiro2016lime}. Attention attribution averages last-layer heads from the CLS token to each position. Integrated-gradients hooks are provided for embedding-space paths. Counterfactual hints list high-risk cues whose removal is expected to lower \(P(y=1)\). Top-\(k\) tokens are verbalized as a rationale.

\subsection{Reward and DPO}
A heuristic reward used for ranking and diagnostics is
\begin{equation}
r = 0.5\,r_{\mathrm{corr}} + 0.3\,r_{\mathrm{faith}} + 0.1\,r_{\mathrm{len}} + 0.1\,r_{\mathrm{style}},
\end{equation}
where \(r_{\mathrm{corr}}\) is label agreement, \(r_{\mathrm{faith}}\) is overlap between post tokens and rationale tokens, \(r_{\mathrm{len}}\) prefers concise explanations, and \(r_{\mathrm{style}}\) penalizes vague or overclaiming text. A learned reward model can replace this heuristic when preference labels are abundant.

Given a frozen reference policy and a trainable policy, let \(\ell_\pi(e\mid x)\) be the mean token log-probability under policy \(\pi\). The implemented length-normalized preference objective minimizes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_\theta(e,x)&=\ell_{\pi_\theta}(e\mid x)-\ell_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(e\mid x), \\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} &=
 -\log\sigma\!\left(\beta[s_\theta(e^+,x)-s_\theta(e^-,x)]\right),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(e^+\) is the chosen explanation and \(e^-\) the rejected one \cite{rafailov2023dpo}. In our implementation the policy is a seq2seq LLM (Flan-T5) prompted to emit \texttt{HATE}/\texttt{NON\_HATE} plus a short justification.

\subsection{Fairness}
Language and code-mix comparisons require adequate held-out support in each group. The benchmark evaluated here carries a single English language designation. The observed error rates are aggregate values, and dialect-level errors require additional annotation \cite{sap2019racial,borkan2019nuanced}.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
We use the published HateXplain splits \cite{mathew2021hatexplain} at a pinned source revision. Of 20,148 source posts, 919 lack a majority among the original \textit{hatespeech}, \textit{offensive}, and \textit{normal} labels and are outside the published splits. We map majority \textit{hatespeech} to the positive class and the other two labels to the negative class. We group canonical exact matches and character-trigram Jaccard matches of at least 0.85 for strings at least 20 characters long. We remove overlapping development rows with priority test, validation, then train, and remove exact same-label duplicates in development. All published test IDs are retained. The final splits contain 15,335 training, 1,920 validation, and 1,924 test posts. HASOC 2021 and Hateful Memes are candidate datasets for subsequent experiments subject to their access terms \cite{mandl2021hasoc,kiela2020hatefulmemes}.

\paragraph{Systems.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Evaluated baseline.} A union of word and character n-gram TF-IDF features with class-balanced logistic regression. Three stratified, group-aware training folds fit sigmoid calibration.
    \item \textbf{Implemented extensions.} XLM-R for text and context, gated fusion with CLIP, and Flan-T5 rationale preference training.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Metrics.}
The binary report uses a probability threshold of 0.5 and includes macro F1, precision, recall, accuracy, the confusion matrix, AUROC, Brier score, and expected calibration error \cite{guo2017calibration}. A separate three-way policy selects a threshold on validation with false negatives weighted five times as heavily as false positives. Posts in a 0.10-wide probability band around that threshold are routed to review \cite{madras2018predict}.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers, with a FastAPI service and a dependency-free HTML/CSS/JavaScript dashboard. Default configs keep vision off so the pipeline runs on CPU; \texttt{configs/cpu.yaml} uses DistilBERT multilingual and Flan-T5-small.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The README specifies the \texttt{hatexplain} data profile and the \texttt{hatexplain\_baseline.yaml} training configuration. The dataset manifest records file checksums and exclusions. We fix the checkpoint and validation-selected policy before opening the test split. The training record logs the manifest and checkpoint checksums, software versions, and fit time. Feedback is stored for human review outside the benchmark inputs.

\paragraph{Human review.}
The validation-selected threshold is 0.202 with a review interval of approximately 0.152 to 0.252. On test, 284 of 1,924 posts (14.8\%) fall in the review band. Accuracy among the 1,640 automatically decided posts is 0.759 under this policy. This coverage-conditioned accuracy reflects both abstention and the change in the probability cutoffs.

\paragraph{Error analysis.}
Among the gold negative posts, the classifier predicts hate for 43 of 782 posts originally labeled \textit{normal} (5.5\%) and 66 of 548 originally labeled \textit{offensive} (12.0\%). The script heuristic detects no mixed-script posts in the test split.

\paragraph{Feature and calibration comparison.}
Three fixed variants trained without calibration achieve test macro F1 of 0.723 for character features, 0.792 for word features, and 0.793 for their union. The calibrated union achieves 0.792 macro F1. Its ECE is 0.020, compared with 0.104 for the uncalibrated union. This comparison measures the full cross-validated calibration procedure, which also averages predictions across fitted folds.

\paragraph{Attribution faithfulness.}
We evaluate the 1,812 test posts with more than five tokens. For each predicted class, we rank tokens by absolute delete-one occlusion score and select the top five. The mean probability drop after removing these tokens is 0.096, compared with 0.031 for five tokens sampled uniformly per post with seed 42. Keeping only the selected tokens in their original order retains mean predicted-class probability of 0.832, compared with 0.757 for the same random control. These perturbation results characterize the baseline's sensitivity to its highlighted tokens.

\paragraph{Limits of the evaluation.}
The public results cover one corpus and one classifier. Public robustness data are needed to quantify errors on quoted hate and counterspeech \cite{gligoric2024use}. The bundled synthetic examples and author-constructed probes remain software diagnostics. Transformer, vision, and DPO comparisons remain open.

\section{Ethics and Limitations}

Hate-speech annotation is culturally situated. A model trained in one community may encode that community's prejudices \cite{sap2019racial}, and abusive-language datasets are themselves biased toward particular topics and dialects \cite{wiegand2019biased}. We do not recommend fully automated penalties. Explanations can be \emph{plausible but unfaithful}; we therefore measure probability drop under masking rather than only human likert scores \cite{jacovi2020faithfulness}. Synthetic data in the repository is for software tests and must not be treated as a demographic sample. Images and user identifiers, if added later, require IRB/ethics clearance and dataset licenses. Dual-use risk: the same features that detect hate can be inverted to write it; we release classifiers and explainers, not generative attack tools.

\section{Conclusion}

HateXAI establishes a reproducible English hate speech baseline and serves its calibrated scores, token attributions, and human-review outcome. The held-out error rates motivate category-specific analysis before operational use. Authorized multilingual and image datasets, together with independently reviewed rationale preferences, are needed to evaluate the remaining components.
