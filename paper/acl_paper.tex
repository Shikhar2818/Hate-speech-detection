\begin{abstract}
Hate speech on social media is multilingual, often code-mixed, increasingly multimodal, and highly context-dependent. High-resource text-only classifiers overfit lexical cues, fail on low-resource and mixed-script posts, and rarely justify decisions in a way moderators can audit. We present \textbf{HateXAI}, a framework that maps a post \(x_i=\{T_i,V_i,C_i\}\) to a binary decision \(y_i\in\{0,1\}\) while emitting a natural-language rationale. A multilingual encoder (XLM-RoBERTa) fuses text and conversational context with optional CLIP visual features through a gated multimodal module. Explanations are produced with LIME, occlusion, and attention attributions, with faithfulness reported as comprehensiveness and sufficiency rather than asserted. A DPO module that aligns generated rationales with chosen versus rejected explanations is implemented and unit-tested, but is \emph{not} trained or evaluated in this work; we therefore separate implemented components from measured ones throughout. What we do measure is a calibrated, CPU-runnable baseline with a cost-weighted three-way decision (hate / non-hate / needs review), an eight-configuration feature and calibration ablation, and an author-constructed adversarial probe set on which the baseline still fails on quoted hate, counterspeech, and neutral identity mentions. We argue that reward-guided rationales plus a human-review outcome are necessary for trustworthy moderation rather than accuracy alone, and we report our system's failures explicitly rather than only its successes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The scale of online conversation has outpaced manual moderation. Automated hate-speech detectors are widely deployed, yet they remain brittle: they are trained mostly on English, they ignore images and thread context, and they behave as black boxes \cite{fortuna2018survey,davidson2017hate,vidgen2020directions}. Errors are not symmetric. False negatives leave targeted groups unprotected; false positives silence minorities whose dialect or reclaimed language is misread as abuse \cite{sap2019racial}.

Low-resource and code-mixed settings make the problem harder. In Indian social media, Hindi--English mixing, romanized Indic text, and culturally specific insults are common, and HASOC-style shared tasks show large drops when systems trained on English are applied off-the-shelf \cite{mandl2019hasoc,macavaney2019hatespeech}. Memes add a second channel in which the text is innocuous but the image supplies the attack \cite{kiela2020hatefulmemes}.

We address three gaps together:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Representation.} Model a post as \(x_i=\{T_i,V_i,C_i\}\) and learn \(f_\theta(x_i)\rightarrow y_i\).
    \item \textbf{Transparency.} Attach token-level and natural-language explanations whose faithfulness can be measured \cite{ribeiro2016lime,jacovi2020faithfulness}.
    \item \textbf{Alignment.} Use preference optimization \cite{rafailov2023dpo} so generated rationales match moderator-preferred explanations rather than fluent but unfaithful text.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A calibrated multilingual and code-mixed classifier with a cost-weighted three-way decision and explicit abstention, evaluated with reliability metrics (Brier, ECE) and a held-out ablation.
(2) An explanation stack (LIME, occlusion, attention, counterfactual cues) whose faithfulness is \emph{measured} by comprehensiveness and sufficiency under a masking procedure shared with the attribution itself.
(3) An inspection-driven audit that identifies and corrects concrete defects, including a self-referential reward score and a misaligned DPO sequence log-probability, with regression tests.
(4) An author-constructed adversarial probe set targeting quoted hate, counterspeech, reclaimed slurs, sarcasm, and non-hateful profanity, with contested cases flagged.
(5) A gated multimodal architecture and a DPO rationale-alignment recipe, both \emph{implemented but not evaluated here}; we report them as such and do not claim results for them.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Hate speech detection.}
Early systems used bag-of-words and character n-grams \cite{davidson2017hate,zampieri2019olid}. Transformer models such as HateBERT improve in-domain English accuracy \cite{caselli2021hatebert} but transfer poorly to code-mixed Hindi--English. HateXplain adds human rationales, enabling explanation-aware training \cite{mathew2021hatexplain}. Implicit hate---abuse that avoids an explicit slur---remains difficult and is benchmarked separately \cite{elsherief2021latent}.

\paragraph{Multilingual and multimodal abuse.}
XLM-R provides strong cross-lingual initialization \cite{conneau2020xlmr}, and production systems now deploy character-level multilingual transformers \cite{lees2022perspective}. HASOC and related FIRE tasks target Indo-European hate and offense \cite{mandl2019hasoc}. The Hateful Memes challenge showed that unimodal models miss image--text interactions \cite{kiela2020hatefulmemes,gomez2020exploring}; CLIP supplies aligned visual features \cite{radford2021clip}.

\paragraph{Explainability.}
LIME \cite{ribeiro2016lime} and SHAP \cite{lundberg2017shap} approximate local decision surfaces. Faithfulness---whether highlighted tokens actually drove the prediction---is distinct from plausibility \cite{jacovi2020faithfulness}. We report comprehensiveness (probability drop after masking top evidence) alongside classification metrics.

\paragraph{Reward-optimized LLMs.}
RLHF trains a reward model and then a policy \cite{ouyang2022instructgpt}. DPO skips explicit RL by optimizing a classification loss on preference pairs \cite{rafailov2023dpo}. We apply DPO not to the hate label alone but to \emph{explanations}, which is closer to how moderators work.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let a social media post be
\begin{equation}
x_i = \{T_i, V_i, C_i\},
\end{equation}
where \(T_i\) is textual content (possibly multilingual or code-mixed), \(V_i\) is an image or meme, and \(C_i\) is context (thread, page, or event metadata). The target is
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(x_i) \rightarrow y_i \in \{0,1\},
\end{equation}
with \(y_i=1\) denoting hateful content. We additionally produce an explanation \(e_i\) and a reward \(r(x_i,e_i,y_i)\).

Hate is treated as \emph{identity-directed abuse or dehumanization}, not merely offense or profanity \cite{davidson2017hate}. This distinction is encoded in preference data: a rejected rationale may call a post hateful because it contains rude words, while the chosen rationale requires a targeted group and harmful intent.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Preprocessing}
URLs and mentions are canonicalized. Language ID and a script-mixing heuristic flag code-mixed posts. Text and context are concatenated with explicit markers so the encoder can attend to \(C_i\) separately from \(T_i\).

\subsection{Multimodal classifier}
XLM-R encodes \(T_i\) and \(C_i\). Optional CLIP image features encode \(V_i\) and are frozen to limit compute. A gated fusion block learns non-negative weights \((\alpha_t,\alpha_v,\alpha_c)\) over projected modalities:
\begin{equation}
h = \alpha_t\,W_t t + \alpha_v\,W_v v + \alpha_c\,W_c c.
\end{equation}
A small MLP maps \(h\) to class logits. Training uses class-weighted cross-entropy, AdamW, and linear warmup.

\subsection{Explainable AI}
For a post \(x\), LIME perturbs tokens and fits a local linear model \cite{ribeiro2016lime}. Attention attribution averages last-layer heads from the CLS token to each position. Integrated-gradients hooks are provided for embedding-space paths. Counterfactual hints list high-risk cues whose removal is expected to lower \(P(y=1)\). Top-\(k\) tokens are verbalized as a rationale.

\subsection{Reward and DPO}
A heuristic reward used for ranking and diagnostics is
\begin{equation}
r = 0.5\,r_{\mathrm{corr}} + 0.3\,r_{\mathrm{faith}} + 0.1\,r_{\mathrm{len}} + 0.1\,r_{\mathrm{style}},
\end{equation}
where \(r_{\mathrm{corr}}\) is label agreement, \(r_{\mathrm{faith}}\) is overlap between post tokens and rationale tokens, \(r_{\mathrm{len}}\) prefers concise explanations, and \(r_{\mathrm{style}}\) penalizes vague or overclaiming text. A learned reward model can replace this heuristic when preference labels are abundant.

Given a frozen reference policy \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) and a trainable policy \(\pi_\theta\), DPO minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} = -\log\sigma\Big(\beta \big[ \log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(e^+|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(e^+|x)} - \log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(e^-|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(e^-|x)} \big]\Big),
\end{equation}
where \(e^+\) is the chosen explanation and \(e^-\) the rejected one \cite{rafailov2023dpo}. In our implementation the policy is a seq2seq LLM (Flan-T5) prompted to emit \texttt{HATE}/\texttt{NON\_HATE} plus a short justification.

\subsection{Fairness}
We report macro-F1 and false-positive rate stratified by language and by code-mix status, following evidence that dialect and identity markers inflate false positives \cite{sap2019racial,borkan2019nuanced}.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
The repository ships a synthetic diagnostic set covering English, Hindi, and Hindi--English code-mix, with paired chosen/rejected rationales. For the camera-ready paper we recommend HateXplain \cite{mathew2021hatexplain} for rationale supervision, HASOC \cite{mandl2019hasoc} for code-mix, and Hateful Memes \cite{kiela2020hatefulmemes} for \(V_i\). A conversion script maps public JSON/JSONL into the project schema.

\paragraph{Systems.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Linear:} character n-gram TF-IDF + logistic regression (CPU baseline).
    \item \textbf{HateXAI-text:} XLM-R with context, no vision.
    \item \textbf{HateXAI-mm:} gated fusion with CLIP.
    \item \textbf{HateXAI-DPO:} Flan-T5 explanations aligned with DPO.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Accuracy, macro-F1, per-class precision/recall/F1, AUROC, confusion matrix, false-positive and false-negative rates; calibration as Brier score and expected calibration error \cite{guo2017calibration}; three-way decision behaviour, including the abstention rate, in the learning-to-defer sense \cite{madras2018predict}; robustness accuracy on the adversarial probe set by category; and explanation comprehensiveness and sufficiency. Threshold-dependent metrics are reported at the operating point selected on validation data under an explicit false-negative/false-positive cost model.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers, with a FastAPI service and a dependency-free HTML/CSS/JavaScript dashboard. Default configs keep vision off so the pipeline runs on CPU; \texttt{configs/cpu.yaml} uses DistilBERT multilingual and Flan-T5-small.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
\texttt{experiments/run\_experiments.py} regenerates every reported number as strict JSON under \texttt{outputs/eval/} together with a run manifest recording seed, device, Python version, and data paths. The decision threshold is selected on the validation split only, and the test split is never used for model or threshold selection. Feedback submitted through the API is stored as a review candidate in a separate append-only log and never enters training or evaluation.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Established (reproducible) results.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the feature and calibration ablation. The legacy character-only baseline reaches 0.667 accuracy and 0.778 AUROC on the bundled test split; selecting by train-only cross-validation yields the union of word and character n-grams with Platt scaling, which raises held-out probe accuracy from 0.639 to 0.750 (unambiguous subset 0.710 to 0.806). Calibration leaves ranking unchanged while reducing Brier score from 0.202 to 0.087.

These numbers are \emph{not} scientific claims. The test split contains six examples and cross-validation covers thirty, so individual differences are not statistically meaningful. The probe set (n=36) is author-constructed rather than a public benchmark.

\paragraph{Failure analysis.}
On the probe set the baseline fails systematically on \emph{quoted} hate speech, \emph{counterspeech} that repeats a slur to condemn it, and \emph{neutral identity mentions}, all of which contain surface cues the classifier treats as evidence of hate. This is the use--mention distinction that automated systems routinely fail and that can be taught \cite{gligoric2024use}; it is the clearest argument for the human-review outcome we provide rather than a forced binary label.

\paragraph{Qualitative analysis.}
Hateful posts that dehumanize a group or call for exclusion receive high \(P(y=1)\) with evidence on target mentions and threat verbs. Non-hateful posts about logistics, sports scores, or civic information remain negative even when they mention identity groups. Code-mixed insults are flagged by character n-grams even when word-level English lexicons miss them.

\paragraph{Error modes.}
We expect residual failures on sarcasm, reclaimed slurs, and memes whose hate is purely visual. DPO can overfit verbose gold rationales if rejected explanations are unrealistically weak; constructing hard negatives (fluent but unfaithful) is essential.

\section{Ethics and Limitations}

Hate-speech annotation is culturally situated. A model trained in one community may encode that community's prejudices \cite{sap2019racial}, and abusive-language datasets are themselves biased toward particular topics and dialects \cite{wiegand2019biased}. We do not recommend fully automated penalties. Explanations can be \emph{plausible but unfaithful}; we therefore measure probability drop under masking rather than only human likert scores \cite{jacovi2020faithfulness}. Synthetic data in the repository is for software tests and must not be treated as a demographic sample. Images and user identifiers, if added later, require IRB/ethics clearance and dataset licenses. Dual-use risk: the same features that detect hate can be inverted to write it; we release classifiers and explainers, not generative attack tools.

\section{Conclusion}

HateXAI couples a multilingual detector with measurable explanation quality and an explicit human-review outcome. Framing posts as \(x_i=\{T_i,V_i,C_i\}\) makes context first-class; the vision and DPO components are implemented but, as this paper states plainly, not yet trained or evaluated. The measured contribution here is a calibrated baseline with a cost-weighted three-way decision, an ablation, and an adversarial probe set that exposes the model's systematic failures on quoted hate and counterspeech. The open codebase, service, dashboard, and ACL draft provide a reproducible student research stack. Next steps are training the transformer and DPO systems on licensed corpora, human evaluation of rationales, and per-language thresholds so false-positive rates stay comparable across groups.
